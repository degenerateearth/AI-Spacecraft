# D1-FAB-001 — launch-inhibit and front-access assembly

Revision A · 2026-09-17  
State: **CUSTOM/TO BE VERIFIED — exact principal parts and logical topology selected; schematic, PCB and mechanical release incomplete**

## Function

D1-FAB-001 provides three physically independent deployer-switch inputs, two
remove-before-flight/service plugs, their protected gate-control circuits, a
target-powered service/programming connector and front-panel access. It works
with D1-EPS-001 and D1-STR-001. It does not carry radio-frequency signals or
provide an external power-injection path.

The assembly is custom. Public BIRDS-3/4 FAB files supply flight-reference
precedent for two switched 2.5 mm RBF jacks and an accessible service board.
Degen-1 does not copy the BIRDS power circuit, assign BIRDS qualification to the
new circuit or treat the public schematic as a mission-specific safety approval.

The 2024 Same Sky jack PDF states 1 A while the current manufacturer product
page states 0.3 A. Degen-1 does not resolve the discrepancy by choosing the
larger value. The jack switch contacts carry only approximately 27–41 µA in the
selected gate network, below either published limit. The removable plug's
conductors are individually insulated and carry no current; plug insertion is
used only to open the jack's internal switch contacts.

Authoritative individual masses are unavailable for several small parts, and
the custom PCB, wiring, brackets, cover and streamers have not been designed.
The assembly mass is therefore **UNKNOWN** and is excluded from any claimed
closed mass budget. Public manufacturer pages provide order routes for the
selected parts, but a controlled, date-stamped price total has not yet been
captured; cost remains a procurement-ledger evidence gap rather than an
invented allowance in this design record.

`SDS002RULC` replaces the earlier `SDS002` selection in D1-EPS-001. The ULC
part has a documented minimum current appropriate to the low-current gate
network. `SiA4265EDJ-T1-GE3` replaces `SQA403EJ-T1_GE3` because its on-resistance
is specified down to -1.8 V gate drive, covering the low-voltage 3S NiMH design
without extrapolating a -4.5 V-only rating.

## Power-path and gate-control definition

The positive battery path is series connected in this order:

```text
D1-BAT-001 positive
  -> QSERV_A <-> QSERV_B       two back-to-back battery-service stages
  -> system battery/charger node
  -> QRBF_A <-> QRBF_B         two back-to-back load-RBF stages
  -> QINH_1 -> QINH_2 -> QINH_3
  -> PROTECTED_BAT_BUS
```

`QSERV_A/B` and `QRBF_A/B` use opposing body-diode orientation so an inserted
plug blocks both current directions after either one of its two stages remains
functional. `QINH_1..3` are oriented to block battery-to-load current. No solar,
EGSE, debug or payload source may connect downstream of these barriers. The
charger battery terminal connects to the system battery/charger node, so the
service pair isolates the physical battery from both charger and loads.

Every MOSFET has its own 100 kΩ gate-to-source resistor. Its gate turns on only
through its own 10 kΩ resistor and corresponding normally closed mechanical
contact. There is no software-controlled parallel turn-on path. The two switch
contacts in each RBF jack control separate series MOSFET stages; they are not
paralleled. Each of the three `SDS002RULC` contacts controls one launch-inhibit
stage. Gate traces remain independent until the intentional ground star at the
board connector. A short of one gate trace must not pull another gate low.

For a 3.0–4.5 V battery allocation, the 100 kΩ/10 kΩ gate network calculates:

| Quantity | At 3.0 V | At 4.5 V | Classification |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Closed-contact current | 27.3 µA | 40.9 µA | CALCULATED from resistor network |
| MOSFET VGS magnitude | 2.73 V | 4.09 V | CALCULATED, ignoring leakage/tolerance |

The current is above the switch's published 10 µA ULC minimum and far below
the contact/jack maximum. At 25 °C, using the MOSFET's 41 mΩ maximum at -2.5 V,
seven on devices produce a preliminary 0.287 Ω path. At the current project
peak allocation of 1.26 A, that is 0.362 V drop and 0.457 W total dissipation.
This is a conservative room-temperature calculation, not a released thermal or
worst-case result. Resistor tolerance, minimum battery voltage, MOSFET leakage,
temperature, copper loss, transient current and radiation remain to be analyzed.

## State and single-fault logic

- Inserted `SERVICE RBF` opens both `QSERV` gate-control contacts and isolates
  the battery from charger and loads.
- Inserted `LOAD RBF` opens both `QRBF` gate-control contacts and isolates all
  protected spacecraft loads.
- In the deployer, each depressed `SDS002RULC` contact is open and commands its
  corresponding `QINH` stage off.
- After both RBF plugs have been removed and all three deployer switches have
  released, the seven series devices may turn on.
- A switch/contact failed open is fail-safe but may prevent commissioning.
- Any one switch contact welded closed or any one series MOSFET failed short
  leaves at least two launch-inhibit stages off in the stowed launch state.
- Either two-channel RBF function remains logically off after one of its two
  controlled MOSFET stages is stuck on.

`verify_inhibit_logic.py` enumerates all eight launch-switch states, plug
override states and the defined single-stuck-on cases. Its JSON output is
CALCULATED Boolean evidence only. It cannot establish electrical independence,
leakage, hardware reliability or launch-provider acceptance.

## Service connector allocation

The ten-position service connector is target-powered and contains no external
power input. Revision-A pin functions are:

| Pin | Net | Direction / rule |
|---:|---|---|
| 1–2 | `GND_SYS` | paired service returns |
| 3 | `UART_SERVICE_TX` | spacecraft output, 3.3 V logic |
| 4 | `UART_SERVICE_RX` | spacecraft input through protection, 3.3 V logic |
| 5 | `ICSP_PGC` | programming clock; protected and isolated from other loads |
| 6 | `ICSP_PGD` | programming data; protected and isolated from other loads |
| 7 | `ICSP_MCLR_VPP` | programming/reset input with manufacturer-required network |
| 8 | `RBF_LOAD_SENSE` | protected high-impedance monitor only |
| 9 | `RBF_SERVICE_SENSE` | protected high-impedance monitor only |
| 10 | `VBAT_MON_BUFFERED` | buffered/scaled monitor, 0–3.3 V; no direct battery path |

An aluminum access cover closes the service opening for flight. The cover,
fasteners, connector restraint, panel cutout and tool clearance remain custom
D1-STR-001/D1-MECH-HW-001 items. No service cable remains installed at launch.

## Mechanical allocation

The three switch actuators shall be mounted at three physically separate rail
locations. Their depressed travel, force, tolerance stack and release margin
shall come from the final deployer interface and the manufacturer drawing; the
project does not assign a final plunger height from nominal dimensions alone.
Each switch PCB/holder is mechanically retained independently of solder joints.
The two RBF jacks and service header are placed behind a recessed front-access
opening and must not violate the rail or protrusion envelope.

Each `SP-2501` plug becomes a custom RBF assembly with insulated unused
conductors, a positive grip, a red streamer marked `REMOVE BEFORE FLIGHT`, a
unique `LOAD` or `SERVICE` label and a controlled removal step. The plastic,
adhesive, ink and streamer do not fly and therefore are not spacecraft
outgassing materials. Their fit and extraction force are acceptance-test items.

## Required release and verification

1. KiCad schematic with seven independent gate networks, ERC report and exact
   passive/protection BOM.
2. Source-to-hazard fault tree including battery, every solar input, charger,
   service connector, data-line backfeed and all MOSFET body diodes.
3. Worst-case gate voltage, leakage, contact-current, voltage-drop and thermal
   calculations over battery voltage, tolerance and temperature.
4. Native PCB/layout release, front-panel drawing, switch-holder drawings and
   full actuator/deployer tolerance stack.
5. Harness drawing for each switch, RBF jack, battery connection and JST service
   cable, including wire, contact, cavity, strain relief and assembly process.
6. All-state bench test with current-limited battery and solar simulators,
   including every switch combination, plug insertion/removal and reset/bounce.
7. Single-fault injections for contact short/open, MOSFET drain-source short,
   gate short, ground short, charger feed and prohibited service backfeed.
8. Post-vibration and thermal-vacuum continuity/isolation/function tests and a
   final mission-ICD reconciliation before launch integration.
