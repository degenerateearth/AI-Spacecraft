# D1-ANT-001 — UHF antenna and deployment assembly

Revision A · 2026-09-16  
State: **CUSTOM/TO BE VERIFIED — architecture and principal parts selected; fabrication release incomplete**

## Controlled selection

Degen-1 uses a custom four-element canted-turnstile antenna on the spacecraft
`-Z` end panel. The electrical starting point is the publicly documented OreSat
436.5 MHz prototype; the retention and resistor-release architecture is derived
from the Quetzal-1 flight antenna-deployment board. Neither upstream design is
claimed as a drop-in Degen-1 assembly.

## RF architecture and dimensional basis

The board accepts one 50-ohm radio feed and uses three `QBA-07+` hybrids plus a
90-degree transmission-line section to create four nominal quadrature feeds.
The line is implemented as controlled-impedance PCB geometry after the final
stack-up is selected; its physical length may not be guessed from free-space
wavelength. Each hybrid isolated port is terminated by one
`FC0402E49R9BST0`.

The OreSat prototype was designed at **436.5 MHz** and measured on a CubeSat
electrical mock-up with a vector network analyzer and in an anechoic chamber.
Its final reported monopole lengths were:

- 0-degree and 180-degree elements: **140 mm** each;
- 90-degree and 270-degree elements: **190 mm** each.

Those four lengths are adopted as Revision-A prototype starting dimensions,
not as validated final Degen-1 tuning. OreSat reports that it pruned the elements
to obtain the match and does not publish the element stock geometry, material,
mount detail or an exact cant angle. Degen-1 therefore makes the following
explicit project decisions for the first controlled prototype:

- element stock: 6.00 mm wide × 0.10 mm thick C17200 beryllium-copper strip,
  solution treated, formed and age hardened under a released supplier process;
- deployed cant angle: 45.0 degrees from the `-Z` end-panel plane;
- alternating 140.0 mm and 190.0 mm active lengths measured from each feed
  reference plane; and
- four 90-degree-spaced pivots centered on the `-Z` panel.

These dimensions are **PROJECT DECISIONS / MODELED INPUTS**, not manufacturer
or flight-proven values. Final element length, pivot position and cant angle
must be established by simulation and full-assembly RF measurement with the
actual structure, solar panels, harness and deployed mechanism. A released
drawing must provide tolerances, bend radii, grain direction, heat treatment,
surface finish, feed attachment and witness-coupon requirements.

The selected `QBA-07+` is a current 50-ohm, 340–680 MHz surface-mount hybrid.
The manufacturer specifies a 6.35 × 7.62 mm body, 1.78 mm nominal height,
0.3 g mass, -55 to +100 °C operating and storage limits, 27 W maximum input,
and 0.5 dB typical insertion loss. These are part properties, not an assembled
antenna gain or link result.

## Release architecture

Each element is elastically stowed against the end panel and restrained by its
own 0.30 mm nylon loop. The loop passes around a dedicated
`MFR50SFTE52-5R6` resistor. A dedicated `TPS22965DSGR` connects the protected
battery bus to that resistor only after launch inhibits and the configurable
deployment delay have cleared. Releases occur one at a time. An
`ESE18L11C` detects each element leaving the stowed position; the
`TCA9539QPWRQ1` reports the four switch states over the protected I2C interface.

This copies the functional chain published and flown by Quetzal-1. Degen-1 uses
a different battery, PCB, structure and antenna element, so Quetzal deployment
success is precedent only.

For the 5.6-ohm resistor and an ideal source, the preliminary electrical points
are `3.6 V / 5.6 ohm = 0.643 A` and `2.31 W`, and `4.5 V / 5.6 ohm = 0.804 A`
and `3.62 W`. Lead, switch, cell and wiring resistance reduce those values.
The Degen-1 battery operating range, pulse duration, resistor hot resistance,
line melt energy and PCB temperature remain open. Firmware shall impose a hard
maximum pulse, verify continuity before firing, wait for switch indication, and
prevent simultaneous releases or radio transmission.

The `ESE18L11C` manufacturer record establishes SPST normally-open operation,
4.0 × 4.4 × 1.2 mm external dimensions, 10 mA at 5 V rating, 300 mN maximum
operating force, 50,000-cycle minimum mechanical life and -40 to +85 °C
manufacturer temperature range. Its actuator must be shown accessible and
unloaded after deployment in the released mechanism.

## Interfaces and keep-outs

1. Mount the assembly on `-Z`; preserve the `+X` camera aperture and field of
   view and all deployer rail/contact keep-outs.
2. The stowed antenna, line, switches and fasteners must remain inside the final
   mission-specific deployer envelope under tolerance and vibration deflection.
3. The four deployed sweeps must clear solar-cell edges, rails, RBF hardware,
   camera field of view and one another, including partial-release states.
4. The RF input is a 50-ohm U.FL interface. D1-HAR-001 must select the exact
   mating cable assembly, connector on the radio carrier, routing, bend radius,
   retention and loss.
5. Release power comes from a separately inhibited protected-battery branch.
   Command/status logic uses 3.3 V. The final fault tree must show that one
   electrical failure cannot cause hazardous early deployment.
6. The element root must provide both a repeatable RF bond and a positive
   mechanical load path. Solder alone is not the launch-load restraint.
7. Total assembly mass and center of mass remain unknown until the PCB,
   elements, pivots, fasteners, line and harness are released.

## Rejected and retained alternatives

- GomSpace `NanoCom ANT430` remains a flight-reference part used by Quetzal-1,
  but it is absent from GomSpace's current public product catalog. Its public
  legacy datasheet does not provide the internal mechanical information needed
  to reproduce or independently analyze the mechanism. It is not the Degen-1
  baseline.
- The earlier ISISPACE 1U AntS candidate is not carried forward because the
  available public product information does not close its internal electrical,
  release, tuning and integration interfaces for independent design release.
- A single dipole would reduce the feed network, but no public Degen-1-specific
  coverage analysis currently shows it provides a more reliable link for the
  passive, variably oriented spacecraft.

## Release and verification path

`D1-ANT-001` remains `CUSTOM/TO BE VERIFIED` until all of the following exist:

1. KiCad schematic, layout, exact stack-up, fabrication data and complete BOM;
2. released element, pivot, stop, retainer, line-routing and end-panel drawings;
3. electromagnetic model with documented materials, mesh convergence and the
   full spacecraft geometry, followed by a link-budget update;
4. structural analysis of stowed and deployed elements, pivots and restraints
   against the selected launch environment;
5. vector-network-analyzer tuning records on the integrated spacecraft or a
   flight-representative RF article;
6. calibrated full-sphere co- and cross-polarized pattern measurements;
7. line-material lot characterization, vacuum/outgassing assessment, resistor
   pulse-energy characterization and bounded hot/cold release timing;
8. repeated ambient, hot, cold and vacuum deployments with nominal, minimum and
   maximum supply conditions and partial-release fault cases;
9. vibration testing followed by stowed continuity and successful deployment;
10. inhibit/delay fault-tree review and end-to-end software/hardware test; and
11. final mission-frequency retuning after spectrum authorization.

The theoretical design can release drawings and analyses before physical tests,
but it may not claim deployment reliability, antenna performance, launch
acceptance or flight readiness without those test results.
